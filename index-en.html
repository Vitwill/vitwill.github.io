If a contractor names a price "for the site" without a breakdown — ask to show the structure. This way you will understand what exactly is included in the work, and what will have to be ordered separately.

Comparison

Agency or Freelancer

Both options work. The difference is in price, speed, level of control, and risks. Here is an honest comparison without marketing.

Agency / Studio

Team · Process · Guarantees
  • Price: 2–4 times higher — you pay for the team, management, and office
  • Speed: slower start (approvals), but parallel work speeds up development
  • Guarantees: contract, warranty period, replacement of the performer if necessary
  • Risks: minimal — but higher price and less flexibility
  • Best for: large projects, tight deadlines, need for reporting

Experienced Freelancer

Flexibility · Direct contact
  • Price: average — without markup for office and management
  • Speed: fast start, direct communication without intermediaries
  • Guarantees: depend on reputation and contract — check reviews and portfolio
  • Risks: higher if the performer disappears — but lower if chosen by recommendation
  • Best for: small and medium businesses, landing pages, business cards, improvements

Main advice: don't choose by price. Choose by portfolio and process transparency. If a performer cannot explain the estimate structure — this is an alarming signal, regardless of whether it's an agency or a freelancer.

Hidden costs

What is forgotten after launch

Development is a one-time investment. After that, the site needs to be maintained. Here are the recurring costs that most clients do not include in the budget — and then are surprised.

01

Hosting

The place where the site physically lives. The quality of hosting affects speed and stability.

$50 – $250per year
02

Domain

The address of the site. Cheaper for .ru, more expensive for international projects .com / .de.

$10 – $30per year
03

Premium plugins

SEO, caching, security, backups. Without licenses, the site works, but less reliably.

$150 – $300per year
04

Support

CMS and plugin updates, fixes, monitoring. Needed if the site is actively used.

$100 – $500per month
05

Content editor

If you plan to publish news or articles yourself — you will need training or help.

$50 – $200per month
06

Speed-up (PageSpeed)

One-time work on optimizing speed and Core Web Vitals. Often required after 6–12 months.

$150 – $400one-time

Total: even a "simple" business card site requires at least $50–$100 per year for hosting and domain alone. If the contractor did not warn you about this — ask yourself. This is a sign of a mature performer.
